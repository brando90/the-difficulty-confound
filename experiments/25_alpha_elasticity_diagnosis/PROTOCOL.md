# Protocol: Experiment 25, why does diluting the target with unrelated data cost only half as much as it should? (`25_alpha_elasticity_diagnosis`)

**Doc link:** <https://github.com/brando90/the-difficulty-confound/blob/main/experiments/25_alpha_elasticity_diagnosis/PROTOCOL.md>

Owner: Laolu Oguneye. Written 11-08-2026, before any run of this experiment. Experiments 11–24 are disclosed as seen, including an exploratory look at Experiment 24's saved results (Section 1.2).

## 1. Why this experiment

### 1.1 The open question

In Experiment 24, even when the non-target data shared nothing with the target (disjoint pools: other hidden Markov model (HMM) languages on tokens 150–299), the target-share elasticity was $e_\alpha = 0.52$, not 1. If only target tokens taught the target, a 20 times smaller share would need a 20 times larger total; it needed about 4.8 times. 13-GPU's S model gave a similar value ($\eta = 0.61$). Every $E = e_\alpha / e_K$ in the series rests on this slope, so before reading $E$ as a fact about learning, we need to know what sets it.

### 1.2 What Experiment 24's saved results already show (exploratory, disclosed)

These were computed on 11-08-2026 from `24_similarity_alignment_complexity/expt_v1/results/` (`tstar.csv`, `metrics.csv`), after Experiment 24's write-up and before this protocol. They shaped the hypotheses and are not evidence for them.

- **The slope is steeper at low α.** Local $-\Delta \ln T^\star / \Delta \ln \alpha$ between neighbouring α, disjoint pools, mean over 3 targets × 3 seeds: S, n = 32: 1.03 (0.01–0.05), 0.55 (0.05–0.2), 0.38 (0.2–0.5), 0.52 (0.5–1). M, n = 32: 0.83, 0.56, 0.27, 0.11. The α = 0.01 cells at n ≥ 32 are mostly right-censored, so their slopes are lower bounds.
- **At n = 8, a little unrelated data makes the target cheaper in total tokens.** Disjoint α = 0.2 crossed sooner than α = 1 in 6 of 9 (target, seed) pairs for S and 7 of 9 for M (M means: ln T* 16.90 vs 17.12).
- **The α = 1 runs start badly.** M, n = 8, mean learning term at $2^{20}$ and $2^{21}$ tokens: 7.5 and 7.6 bits/token at α = 1, against 2.8 and 1.7 at disjoint α = 0.2. Experiment 24's single learning rate (4× the 13-GPU table, chosen by a pilot at α = 0.2 and the top of its grid) may be too high for single-concept data.
- **The curves flatten late.** S, n = 32, disjoint α = 0.01: 0.185, 0.169, 0.148 bits at $2^{26}$, $2^{27}$, $2^{28}$. Training used a constant learning rate after 200 warmup steps.

### 1.3 Hypotheses

Each one predicts a different pattern across the arms of Section 2.2.

| | Hypothesis | Signature |
|---|---|---|
| **H1** | **One learning rate for all α.** The rate that suits mixed data is too high for target-only data, so α = 1 is slowed and the slope flattens | $e_\alpha$ rises when each cell gets its own best rate (G1) |
| **H2** | **Batches larger than needed.** A step on 128 target documents teaches little more than a step on fewer, so diluting the batch costs less than proportionally | with distractors that give no gradient at all, $e_\alpha < 1$ (G2) |
| **H3** | **Transfer of structure.** Disjoint HMMs still teach how to track a hidden state in context, which the target reuses | distractors that give gradient help more than distractors that give none (G3), and the help goes when the distractors lose their sequential structure (G4) |
| **H4** | **A share-independent part of the bill.** Some of the cost (generic statistics, warm-up) is paid by any tokens, so $T^\star \approx T_0 + T_1/\alpha$ | $e_\alpha$ larger below α = 0.2 than above (G5) |

They are not exclusive. Several gates can pass.

## 2. Design

### 2.1 Targets and pools (Experiment 24's, unchanged)

The 6 targets are Experiment 24's at n = 8 and n = 32 (3 each, `hmm_n008_t1`–`t3`, `hmm_n032_t1`–`t3`). Each keeps its 4 **disjoint** distractors (same n, tokens 150–299, certified rank, entropy rate inside the targets' band). Both are regenerated by Experiment 24's unchanged code (`concepts24.build_all`) from its frozen config, with the same task ids and concept ids, so every document is generated exactly as in Experiment 24. The check-only step asserts that the definitions and ids equal Experiment 24's committed `results/tasks.json`. n = 64 is dropped because its α ≤ 0.05 cells were censored in Experiment 24, and this question does not need it.

### 2.2 Arms: what the non-target documents are

| Arm | Non-target documents | Loss on them |
|---|---|---|
| `hmm` | the 4 disjoint HMMs (Experiment 24's disjoint arm) | yes |
| `iid` | for each disjoint HMM, a one-state source emitting its exact document unigram (the average token distribution over positions 1–128 from a uniform start, rounded to multiples of $2^{-12}$, every token of its support kept, total variation ≤ 0.02): the same tokens with the same frequencies, and no sequential structure. Its entropy rate is higher than its parent's (4.91 vs 2.88 bits/token, mean of the 24), because the structure that makes the next token predictable is gone | yes |
| `masked` | the same 4 disjoint HMMs, the same documents | **no**: the loss is the mean over the target documents' tokens in the batch, so distractor documents give no gradient. This equals target-only training with about $128\alpha$ target documents per step and the same number of steps |

The α = 1 cell has no distractors, so it is shared by all three arms.

### 2.3 Share, learning rate and the grid

- $\alpha \in \{0.02, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.5, 1\}$, with Experiment 13's exact document rule (document $k$ is a target document iff $\lfloor (k+1)\alpha \rfloor - \lfloor k\alpha \rfloor = 1$; distractor slots cycle through a seeded permutation of the pool). Every batch of 128 documents holds at least 2 target documents (checked), so the masked arm is never empty.
- **Learning rate:** 0.5×, 1×, 2×, 4× and 8× the 13-GPU table rate (S 1e-3, M 6e-4). 4× is Experiment 24's choice (the reference). There is no pilot: the rate is a factor of the grid.
- Seeds `25101, 25202, 25303` (new; initialization and data stream).
- Grid: 2 sizes × 6 targets × 16 cells (α = 1, plus 3 arms × 5 α < 1) × 5 learning rates × 3 seeds = **2,880 runs**, one GPU job (shard) each. Shard id = (((i_size · 6 + i_target) · 16 + i_cell) · 5 + i_lr) · 3 + i_seed.

### 2.4 Models, training and evaluation (Experiment 24's)

- Models: S (4 layers, d 256, 3,159,552 non-embedding parameters) and M (8 layers, d 512, 25,220,096), vocabulary 301.
- Training: Experiment 13-GPU's code (`gpu_arm.py`) as in Experiment 24. AdamW (β 0.9, 0.95), weight decay 0.1 on matrices, gradient clipping 1.0, 200 warmup steps, then constant, batches of 128 documents, bfloat16 autocast, the same initialization for every run of one seed and size. Every run trains to $T = 2^{28}$ tokens, with checkpoints at every doubling from $2^{20}$. $T$ counts every token in the batch, masked ones included, so $T$ is proportional to steps in every arm.
- **Divergence:** if a checkpoint's code lengths are not finite, the run stops there (recorded as `diverged_at`). A diverged run that had not crossed $\varepsilon^\star$ is right-censored at $2^{28}$.
- Evaluation at every checkpoint, float64, deterministic: 1,024 held-out documents per target (Experiment 24's own held-out documents: same eval seed and concept ids). Excess ε over the exact oracle; identification floor $\varepsilon_{\text{id}}$ = the exact Bayes mixture of target and the arm's pool under the training prior (α, (1 − α)/4 each) for `hmm` and `iid`, and 0 for `masked` (it learns from target documents only); learning term $\varepsilon_{\text{learn}} = \varepsilon - \varepsilon_{\text{id}}$, per slice and per position. Plus 64 documents per pool member (distractor loss, reported).

## 3. Measurements

**The data bill** $T^\star$ is the first crossing of $\varepsilon^\star = 0.1$ bits/token by $\varepsilon_{\text{learn}}$ (all positions), log-log interpolated between checkpoints (Experiment 24's rule). Right-censored if never reached by $2^{28}$, left-censored if already below at $2^{20}$.

## 4. Analysis

### 4.1 The learning-rate policies

- **Reference:** every run at 4× (Experiment 24's setting).
- **Tuned:** for each (size, n, arm, α) cell, the multiplier with the lowest mean $\ln T^\star$ over its 3 targets × 3 seeds (a censored run counts at its bound; ties go to the smaller multiplier). All 9 runs of the cell at that multiplier enter the fit. The α = 1 cell is tuned once per (size, n). The choice is redone inside every bootstrap draw, on the draw's weights. A choice at the edge of the grid (0.5× or 8×) is reported per cell.

### 4.2 Primary estimator

Per arm and policy, Experiment 24's Tobit (censored normal) regression of $\ln T^\star$ on that arm's runs plus the shared α = 1 runs, sizes pooled (size intercepts, common slopes):

$$\ln T^\star = \mu_{\text{size}} - e_\alpha \ln \alpha + e_K \ln n + \sigma\epsilon .$$

**Hinge fit** (for G5): the same with an extra column $\min(\ln\alpha - \ln 0.2, 0)$, whose coefficient $c$ gives $e_\alpha^{\text{high}} = e_\alpha$ (α ≥ 0.2) and $e_\alpha^{\text{low}} = e_\alpha - c$ (α ≤ 0.2). Maximum likelihood (BFGS from the least-squares start). An independent fit (L-BFGS-B from zero) must agree to $10^{-4}$ relative on every coefficient.

### 4.3 Uncertainty

95% percentile intervals from Experiment 24's two-stage bootstrap: resample the 3 seeds, then the 3 targets within each level, with replacement, 20,000 draws, analysis seed `25110826`. Degenerate draws are counted and reported. **Every-seed rule:** each gate's point condition must also hold when the analysis (policy choice included) is run on each seed alone.

### 4.4 Gates (all tuned unless stated; each also needs the every-seed rule)

| Gate | Claim | SUPPORTED iff |
|---|---|---|
| **G1** | One rate for all α biases $e_\alpha$ down (H1) | $e_\alpha(\texttt{hmm}, \text{tuned}) - e_\alpha(\texttt{hmm}, 4\times) > 0$ with 95% lower bound > 0 |
| **G2** | Target-only learning is not purely token-limited (H2) | $e_\alpha(\texttt{masked}) < 1$ with upper bound < 1. Named opposite outcome: "$e_\alpha(\texttt{masked}) > 1$" (lower bound > 1 and every seed > 1) |
| **G3** | Disjoint HMM distractors substitute for target data (H3) | $e_\alpha(\texttt{masked}) - e_\alpha(\texttt{hmm}) > 0$ with lower bound > 0. Named opposite outcome: "interference" (difference < 0, upper bound < 0, every seed < 0) |
| **G4** | The substitution comes from sequential structure (H3) | $e_\alpha(\texttt{iid}) - e_\alpha(\texttt{hmm}) > 0$ with lower bound > 0 |
| **G5** | Part of the bill is share-independent (H4) | $e_\alpha^{\text{low}} - e_\alpha^{\text{high}} > 0$ (`hmm`, hinge fit) with lower bound > 0 |

### 4.5 Reported, not gated

- $e_\alpha$, $e_K$, $E$ for every arm at every fixed multiplier, tuned per size, and the hinge for `iid` and `masked`.
- The tuned multiplier per cell, and which cells chose a grid edge.
- The early learning term at $2^{20}$, $2^{21}$, $2^{22}$ per (size, n, arm, α, multiplier): the α = 1 start-up penalty of Section 1.2.
- The gate statistics at $\varepsilon^\star = 0.05$ and 0.2; censoring counts per cell; diverged runs; Experiment 23's convergence rule per cell; $\varepsilon_{\text{id}}$ and the posterior after 16 tokens per pool and α; distractor loss at $2^{28}$.

### 4.6 What each outcome would mean

- **G1 only:** Experiment 24's 0.5 is mostly an artifact of tuning the rate at α = 0.2. Earlier $E$ values that used one rate for all α should be re-read with per-cell rates.
- **G2 SUPPORTED, G3 NOT_ESTABLISHED:** dilution is cheap because a full batch of target documents is more than a step needs. Nothing is learned from the unrelated data. $e_\alpha$ then depends on the batch size, which is a training choice, not a property of the target.
- **G3 and G4 SUPPORTED:** unrelated HMMs teach a skill the target uses (tracking a hidden state), and the skill needs their sequential structure. "Disjoint" data is not irrelevant in this toy.
- **G5 SUPPORTED:** a fixed part of the bill is paid by any tokens, so $e_\alpha$ is not one number: it approaches 1 at small shares. Fits of one constant slope over α ∈ [0.01, 1] understate it at the small shares that matter in pretraining.
- **No gate SUPPORTED:** the half-price dilution survives every control here. It is not the learning rate, the batch, transfer of structure, or a fixed cost of this kind.

## 6. Verification (`verify25.py`)

- **Per shard, on its GPU right after training:**
  - every file in DONE matches its SHA-256;
  - the shard ran the current sources and prep, at its grid learning rate;
  - the training stream regenerates over the steps run (SHA-256 and per-checkpoint document counts);
  - every checkpoint replays in float64 to < $10^{-9}$ bits (per slice and per position).

  It writes VERIFIED. Then every checkpoint except $2^{28}$ is deleted (a diverged run keeps its last finite one). Their SHA-256s stay in DONE and are listed in VERIFIED.
- **Merged, on CPU:**
  - every concept regenerates (Experiment 24's code and config; the iid sources from their parents) with certified ranks and recomputed $K_{\text{bits}}$;
  - held-out documents regenerate;
  - oracle and both mixtures agree with the log-space forward algorithm to $10^{-9}$;
  - all 2,880 shards carry a matching VERIFIED marker;
  - the analysis reruns to identical point estimates and verdicts;
  - the independent Tobit fitter agrees.

## 7. Compute, hardware and the cost cuts

- **Estimate:** Experiment 24's measured wall times on an H200 were a median of 173 s per S run and 367 s per M run. That gives about 69 + 147 ≈ **216 GPU-hours** for 2,880 runs, and more when GPUs are shared. Prep (CPU) takes about 5 minutes.
- **Where:** SNAP skampere2 only. skampere1 is not used at all: other runs there take precedence. A job starts only on a GPU with ≥ 24 GB free (worst of 3 samples), re-checked before every job. A GPU may carry up to 3 of our jobs and may be busy with other users' work if the memory rule holds (Laolu's sharing rule). At most 12 of our jobs run at once. These limits are runtime knobs in `state/` and may be lowered at any time; raising them above these values is a deviation and must be logged.
- **Cost cuts adopted** (negligible effect on results):
  1. Keeping only the $2^{28}$ checkpoint after replay. Storage only: every checkpoint is verified before deletion. Kept: about 165 GB (M 102 MB, S 13 MB per run).
  2. Dropping n = 64, and α = 0.01 (censored at n = 32 in Experiment 24); α = 0.02 and 0.1 are added for the curvature.
- **Rejected:** early stopping, fewer learning rates (the tuned policy needs the optimum inside the grid), and fewer seeds.
- **Storage:** `/dfs/scratch1/laoluog1/tdc_exp25/`.

## 9. Limits

- Synthetic languages only, two model sizes, six targets, three seeds.
- The learning rate is tuned on a 5-point grid, and only the peak rate. The warmup and constant schedule are fixed, so a schedule effect (decay to zero) is not tested here.
- The masked arm changes the number of target documents per step and nothing else. It does not separate batch-size effects from optimizer-state effects (Adam's moment estimates see only target gradients).
- The `iid` arm removes all sequential structure at once, and with it raises the distractors' entropy (4.91 vs 2.88 bits/token). It does not grade structure, and it does not separate "no structure to transfer" from "noisier distractors".

**TLDR-end:** [tdc: Expt 25 protocol] Find out why diluting a target with unrelated data costs half what it should ($e_\alpha \approx 0.5$ in Experiment 24). Each cell gets its own learning rate. Distractors that teach nothing (masked loss) and unigram-only distractors are compared with Experiment 24's disjoint HMMs, and the slope is tested for steepening at small shares. 2,880 runs on two transformer sizes, gates G1–G5.
