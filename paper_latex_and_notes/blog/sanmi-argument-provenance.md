## User-supplied original message

The user pasted the following message attributed to Brando, dated 06-06-2026 at 11:00 AM in the pasted conversation. The original conversation platform and timezone were not independently verified. Spelling is preserved.

> I have a contrarian take on "this difficulty" hypothesis. Models are train on data. As we know. So if they are trained a lot on the "difficult data" then it actually becomes easier (since it's in distribution). So personally I'm always skeptical on difficulty claims due to this.
> ps: obviously it doesn't mean I don't believe difficulty isn't a thing. But there are confounders...

Sanmi's concern, as supplied by the user, was whether the measured construct remains stable as benchmark difficulty rises; his response recognized that difficulty depends on population and context. The blog summarizes that discussion without claiming access to its original source.

## Later user-supplied restatement

The current user message pasted a later exchange with Varun and Brando's stronger formulation: “there isn't such a thing as ‘difficulty’ — only aligment matters,” motivated partly by training on data of high Kolmogorov complexity. The pasted exchange has times but no verified date. The blog renders this as an intentionally provocative hypothesis and explains why it cannot be treated as a universal conclusion.

The pasted claim that the experiments were on reinforcement learning is corrected in the draft: the completed runs are supervised addition. The relevance to reinforcement-learning environments and Varun's predictive laws remains a proposed extension. Varun suggested eventual Anarres integration, and Brando said to discuss it at their one-to-one; this task does not copy the project into Anarres.
